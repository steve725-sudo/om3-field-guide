The OM-3 stores a complete camera inside each custom slot — exposure, focus, buttons, display, and its own set of colour and mono recipes. Learn the structure once and the 400 menu items stop mattering.

How the camera fits together

1. The mode dial: C1 to C5

C1 Landscape/LE, C2 Birds in flight, C3 Street, C4 Macro, C5 Astro. Each slot stores roughly 400 settings. While you are still tuning a slot, set Save Settings = Hold so tweaks stick; once happy, set it to Reset so accidental changes are thrown away when you leave the mode.

Trap: B (Bulb/Live Time) inherits settings from M, not from C1. Before a long-exposure session, recall C1 into main memory (Reset/Custom Modes → Recall), then work between M and B so ISO and aperture don't jump.

2. The Fn lever: what the dials do

Position 1 — front dial shutter speed, rear dial exposure compensation. Used for BIF and Street with Auto ISO, so you can rescue a blown or dark bird in one twist.

Position 2 — front dial shutter speed, rear dial aperture. Used for everything else, and to pre-set aperture before flicking back to position 1.

Landscape, Macro and Astro default to ISO 200; BIF and Street default to Auto ISO (ceiling 6400, 12800 for BIF).

3. The CP button

One button replaces four on the OM-1. Press CP and turn the front dial to cycle High-Res, Live ND, HDR, Focus stacking and Live GND. Bracketing (BKT) is not on CP, so it goes on the Rec button where needed.

4. Back-button focus and the half-press

AF-ON does the focusing. In Landscape and Macro the shutter half-press is set not to refocus (AF by half-pressing = No), so once you've manually focused, pressing the shutter won't undo it. In BIF and Street the half-press focuses normally, because the front button may be busy with Pro Capture.

5. The noise-reduction trick for long exposures

NR can't be put on a button. So: Silent-shutter settings → NR Off; main Noise Reduction → Auto. Custom modes shoot silent (no NR, fast test shots); Bulb/Live Time always uses mechanical shutter (NR on, clean final frame). Test fast, shoot clean.

Cards

Slot 1: fast 64 GB, used for BIF and Street (high frame rates, offloaded and formatted each day). Slot 2: 256 GB, used for Landscape and Macro (low volume, rarely formatted).

    Looks

    A look is one recipe for the Creative Dial. The OM-3 exposes the Pen-F colour engine: twelve hue saturations numbered 0–11 clockwise from Yellow (0 Yellow, 1 Yellow-Red, 2 Red, 3 Red-Magenta, 4 Magenta, 5 Magenta-Blue, 6 Blue, 7 Blue-Cyan, 8 Cyan, 9 Green-Cyan, 10 Green, 11 Green-Yellow), a three-point curve, sharpness, contrast, and — in COLOR mode — four slots that save to a custom mode. These are the community's best recipes, drawn as colour wheels so you can read a look before you dial it in.

    Loading a recipe

    1. Creative Dial to COLOR (or MONO), press OK, pick the base profile the recipe names.
    2. Press INFO to reach the colour wheel; set the 12 values clockwise from Yellow (position 0).
    3. Next screen: Shadow / Midtone (INFO) / Highlight, then Shading, Sharpness, Contrast.
    4. White balance and the A/G or A/M shift live outside the profile — set them on the SCP.
    5. Save the whole state to a spare C-slot. The WB shift only survives that way.

    Shift axes: OM-3 uses A (amber) and M (magenta); the older Pen-F recipes use A and G (green). G+2 ≈ M−2.

    Slot planner

    What this page is for. The Settings tab tells you how each slot behaves (exposure, focus, buttons). This page adds the second half: which looks live inside that slot. A slot keeps its own four colour and four mono profiles, so C3 Street can carry a Kodachrome and a Tri-X that C1 Landscape never sees. Choose them here, press Build loading sequence, and you get the exact order to dial everything into the camera and save it to the slot. Saved on this device.

    Discipline: more than two colour and one mono per slot and you'll stop recognising them in the field. The grid lets you fill eight; it doesn't mean you should.

    What to try

    1. Lever 2, sunny beach, ISO 200, f/5.6. Turn the front dial until the sun just blinks red. That is ETTR.
    2. Now slow the shutter to 1/8 for the water. Watch it blow out. Add Live ND 64 and bring it back.
    3. Switch to Dusk. Note how much shutter you need, then add GND 3 and see the sky come down while the rocks stay.
    4. Lever 1: ISO goes Auto, the rear dial becomes exposure compensation. Turn it +1 and watch the ISO climb instead of the shutter.

    Modelled, not measured: the scene is synthetic and the numbers are textbook exposure maths. The relationships are what you are learning here, not absolute values.
